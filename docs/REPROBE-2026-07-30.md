# Main-image re-probe sweep, 2026-07-30 (Mercury, 18-core host)

> **`work/` is in `.gitignore`.** The drafts this session produced
> (`work/reprobe-2026-07-30/{drafts,reordered,pipeline,fresh,solved}/`) are
> therefore **local to the machine that made them and are not in the repository**.
> The measurements, floors and levers are reproduced here and in `HANDOVER.md`
> because those are the parts that survive; the per-stem floors and their winning
> flags are in `docs/reprobe-2026-07-30-floors.tsv` and
> `docs/reprobe-2026-07-30-mode-matrix.tsv`, both tracked. Regenerate any draft
> with `tools/finish_draft.sh` from the stem's best-known source.

What HANDOVER.md §10 predicted would be the largest recoverable backlog on a
high-compute host — a mechanical re-probe of every existing park note against the
modes those notes predate — was run to exhaustion here. **It is nearly dry on the
main image.** This file records the measurement so nobody spends the cores again.

## Population

- 1,035 stems have a draft somewhere under `work/` *and* still have an
  `asm/<stem>.s`, i.e. drafted but unadopted.
- 3,188 unique `(stem, content)` drafts after content-deduplication, capped at 30
  per stem (a few permuter-heavy stems hold thousands of near-identical states:
  `080a1870` alone has 13,559).
- 1,355 of those compile; **964 compile *and* pass the policy screen**;
  303 distinct stems.

## The policy screen is not optional

`work/hand/080044d0/exact/` and `.../exact_barrier/` both score
**0 differing halfwords at 212/212** and are both **inadmissible**: they reach it
with `register s32 ratio asm("r1")`, which `PROVENANCE.md` forbids outright (no
`asm()`, no register pinning, no barriers). Its best *clean* variant is
`work/hand/080044d0/080044d0.c` at **1 halfword, size-exact** — a pure register
identity residual.

Any sweep that ranks drafts by score alone will surface pinned drafts at the top
of its list and read as a pile of free adoptions. Screen for
`asm(`, `register … asm`, `volatile`, `__attribute` before believing a zero.

## Result of the full mode matrix

964 clean drafts × 20 flag settings = **19,280 probes in 122 s**. The settings
covered each of §7's eight routed modes alone, both CSE modes paired, and the

**Exactly one exact hit: `08078144`, 228 bytes, adopted this session.** It

## `-fsched-low-dest-first` and the paired CSE modes are not the main-image lever

No note anywhere under `work/` mentions `-fsched-low-dest-first`,
`-fno-cse-two-insn-immediate` or `-fno-cse-pool-immediate` — the corpus wholly
predates them, which is exactly the stale-evidence shape §6 describes. The
inference that they must therefore be holding back a batch of main-image
functions was **tested and is false**: across 964 drafts they produced no
additional exact match. They still move individual counts (see the TSV), so they
remain worth probing per function; they are not a backlog.

## Host concurrency, measured

18 concurrent probe processes on an 18-core M5 Max: **load average 25.6** while
per-probe latency stayed at **~114 ms against a 116 ms idle baseline**. §10's
falsifiable stopping signal is probe latency past ~150 ms, and it never
triggered, so load average well above core count is *not* the ceiling here. The
4-core host's "roughly one lane per core" rule understates this machine; the
latency signal is the one to trust.

## The permuter silently rejected every modern hand draft

`permute_v1.ts` prepended `M2C_PREAMBLE` unless a draft started with `typedef`.
Hand drafts start with `#include "types.h"`, so they got duplicate typedefs and
died in `xgcc`, reported only as `probe-failed … unusable`. The split was exact:
of 10 near-miss targets, the **6 beginning `#include` all failed** and the
**4 beginning `typedef` all scored**. Anyone reading that output would conclude
the permuter cannot work on main-image targets.

Fixed by deciding the preamble per draft (`selfContained()`) and carrying it with
the chosen body rather than assuming it globally. Re-running the same 10 targets
then **matched `080f7f30` (72 bytes)**, which was previously scored and parked at
floor 9 — the tool had been searching a corrupted source for that target and
never seeing the others at all.

Note the permuter's floors are **not comparable to `candidate_show` counts**: it
scores with a weighted edit distance, so e.g. `08022768` reads 9 there against 3
halfwords here. Do not inherit a permuter floor as a park.

## What the non-compiling remainder actually is

Of 3,188 deduplicated drafts, 1,833 do not compile, and **789 of those are raw
`work/matches/m2c/` output**. They fail on m2c's own artifacts — the sampled
errors are `subroutine_arg0' undeclared` and similar — not on anything a flag or
a spelling fixes. So the 689 stems that have a draft but no *compiling* draft are
**drafting work, not mechanical work**: someone has to write the C. Do not budget
them as a sweep.

## Statement-order lever: now tooled, and its limit is the dependency test

Section 4 records that store order moves register allocation (allocno priority is
`2*refs/live_length`, and a parameter's store position sets its live length), and
that on one function 24 orderings were tried by hand and two gave zero where the
obvious ordering gave 15. Nothing automated it, so every
"register-identity-only swap" park was triaged without the lever having been
swept. `tools/statement_order_sweep_main.ts` does it now: it permutes maximal runs
of *independent* top-level statements, greedily keeps improvements, and repeats to
a fixpoint.

Swept 53 owned size-exact targets for **10,982 probes**. No zeros. Five drafts
improved and are saved under `reordered/`: `080c1fa8` to 5 halfwords,
`08016178` to 17, `08004278` to 18, `080a6a98` to 11, `080a22f4` to 21.

**Its real limit is the independence test, not the search.** Two statements that
both touch memory or call a function are treated as ordered, because source alone
cannot prove they do not alias. On pointer-heavy drafts that leaves almost nothing
permutable: `08077394` produced 1 probe, `080ae99c` 3, `0808b868` 7,
`080a8578` 12. Where the run is genuinely independent it is exhaustive
(`08011fd8` took all 721 orderings of its run and confirmed a real floor). So a
null from this tool means either "no permutable run" or "permutation does not
reach it", and the probe count in the log tells you which. Read the count before
recording a park.

A safe extension, if someone wants it: use the region's own disassembly to prove
two memory operations disjoint, then admit them into a run. That is more evidence
than the source carries, and it is the only way to widen this lever.

## 080c1fa8: 5 to 2 by hand, and the last two are a well-specified sched problem

Worked as coordinator while the lanes ran. Two levers, both from section 4, each
measured separately:

1. **`out = buffer;` belongs after the pointer derivations, not before.** The
   reference emits `adds r2,r3,#1` / `adds r0,#11` / `adds r4,r6,#0`, so the
   buffer-base copy is last of the three. Moving that one statement: 5 to 4.
2. **A two-address `muls` takes its operand order from the source.** Ours emitted
   `adds r3,r0,#0 / muls r3,r5` against the reference's `adds r3,r5,#0 /
   muls r3,r0`. Spelling the product `Func_08004458() * count` instead of
   `count * Func_08004458()` fixes it: 5 to 3 on its own, and **2 combined**.
   Section 4 notes two-address `muls` avoids a copy only as `x = a & mask;
   x *= scale;`; this is the neighbouring rule, that for a call-result product the
   *written* operand is whichever factor is spelled first.

The improved draft is `reordered/080c1fa8.c` and it no longer needs
`-fno-sched-depend-count` (2 at baseline). The remaining residual is a genuine
ordinal tie-break:

```
! 0016  adds r0, r3, #0   |   mov  r6, sp
! 0018  mov  r6, sp       |   adds r0, r3, #0
```

The reference materialises the stack base one slot earlier. Measured against all
40 flag settings plus every pairing with `-fno-sched-depend-count`, three further
statement placements, a declaration-initialiser spelling, and a return-type sweep:
**nothing moves it.** This belongs in HANDOVER section 8's compiler lane as a
`mov rN,sp` versus argument-setter ordinal tie-break, distinct from the r0-r3 case
`-fsched-low-dest-first` already covers.

## A dark fork mode, found by reading the compiler binary

A lane reported a best-mode string containing `-fthumb-group-value2-in-place`,
which is in no routing set and in no section of HANDOVER. It turned out to exist
in the **pinned** `cc1`, documented in the fork's own `flags.h`, and to be absent
from both `tools/alchemy_gcc.ts` and `FORK_MODES` in `tools/mode_sweep.ts` — so no
sweep in this repo could ever name it.

Found the rest by diffing the binary's option strings against `tools/`:

```sh
strings toolchain/alchemy-gcc/cc1 | grep -oE '^(thumb-[a-z0-9-]+|grouped-dma-store)$' | sort -u
```

Of 26 fork options, `-fthumb-group-value2-in-place` was the **only** one referenced
nowhere in `tools/`. Three others (`thumb-and-sets-cc`, `thumb-load-latency-one`,
`entry-low-register-order`) are in `FORK_MODES` but unrouted, which is the normal
state for a candidate mode.

Swept the dark mode plus those three over 300 clean drafts (9,180 probes, 42 s).
No exact hits from the sweep itself, and exactly one floor moved: **`080b5ad4`,
28 halfwords at 68/64 → 9 at 64/64**. Then the §4 epilogue rule finished it: the
reference's `pop {r1} / bx r1` tail means r0 is live, so declaring the function
`s32` and spelling the tail `return Func_080072f0(...)` reaches **0**. Adopted at
64 bytes. Without the mode, the best any of the 40 other flag settings reaches is
28 at the wrong size, so the mode is genuinely load-bearing here, not cosmetic.

The instructive part is why it was dark. The routing comment above
`GROUPED_DMA_STORE_SOURCES` already stated this function's blocker exactly —
"value0 has a special case in that pass and value2 has none" — and the fork had
since implemented that special case. Correct when written, stale when read. The
lesson generalises: **when a routing comment names a missing compiler capability,
check whether the fork has grown it since.**

## Measured: cheap-model breadth against strong-model depth

The question was whether many cheap agents beat fewer strong ones on this work.
Four haiku lanes were given four size-exact near-misses with the residual
fingerprint quoted for them, the lever sections named, and the two mechanical
sweeps pointed out.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `080ae99c` | 84 | 9 | 9 | 20 | 6.3 min |
| `08077394` | 68 | 8 | 8 | 11 | 6.5 min |
| `080a8578` | 140 | 7 | 7 | 16 | 7.0 min |
| `0801965c` | 104 | 7 | 7 | 19 | 8.1 min |

**0 of 4 improved**, 66 spellings total, about 90k tokens per lane. Every report
was honest and well-formed, and one correctly noticed that the statement-order
sweep had only produced 3 probes on its target, so the lever had never actually
been exercised there. The failure was not diligence or obedience; it was that
none of the 66 spellings was the right lever.

**The comparison is not controlled and should not be read as a clean model
benchmark.** These four were the residual-hard remainder: I had already failed on
`080a8578` and `0801965c` myself at full effort, and only moved `080c1fa8`
5 → 2 without closing it. The opus lanes were given a different, easier mix.
What this does establish is that a quoted fingerprint plus a lever list is not
enough to substitute for lever selection, which is the expensive cognitive step.

**The load-bearing conclusion is about neither model.** The cheapest search all
session was no agent at all: 38,480 flag probes in ~4 minutes at 114 ms each,
plus 9,180 more in 42 s, which between them produced 2 of the 8 conversions. An
agent probing 20 spellings in 6 minutes is roughly 4 orders of magnitude less
search per minute than `xargs`. So the division of labour is:

- **enumerable** (flag sets, callee return types, statement permutations,
  constant spellings): `xargs` over `candidate_show`, never an agent;
- **non-enumerable** (choose which lever the residual implies, decode assembly
  into semantics, notice the epilogue contradicts the signature): a strong model.

Spending agent turns on the first kind is the real waste, independent of tier.

## The frontier, measured correctly

## tools/reference_shape.ts

Reads each region's epilogue and reports the return type §4 says it proves, for
every region at once, plus every draft whose declared type contradicts it. Free
facts that were being rediscovered by byte diff.

Over 1,131 decidable regions: 425 void, 255 value-returning, 45 `bx lr` leaves,
406 not decidable from the tail. It found **12 compiling clean drafts whose

## A verifier confirmed a draft that violated PROVENANCE

`080060e8`'s verify lane reported `confirmed=true`. It reached 0 halfwords, but
its source declared `volatile u16 *timer = (volatile u16 *)0x0400010e;` and
`*(volatile u16 *)0x0400010e = 0xC0;`. The verifier appears to have accepted the
`volatile` as legitimate memory-mapped-I/O semantics, which is a defensible
reading for a real GBA timer register.

The coordinator's independent grep caught it, and then the decisive test settled
it: **the draft reaches 0 without `volatile` at all**, so it was never
load-bearing. Adopted volatile-free.

Two rules follow, and they are cheap:
1. **A verifier must not adjudicate `volatile` on intent. It must delete it and
   re-probe.** If the count is unchanged the `volatile` was noise; if the count
   worsens, then it is a matching device and §9's prohibition applies, MMIO
   argument or not.
2. **Never adopt on a lane's `confirmed` alone.** Re-screen and re-measure at the
   coordinator. This is the second policy escape caught this way today, the first
   being the register-pinned `080044d0` drafts.

## 08006cdc: the volatile question, answered the other way

A second lane's zero also failed the coordinator's policy screen, and this one is
the opposite case to `080060e8`. `08006cdc` (116 bytes) writes
`0x0E005555`/`0x0E002AAA` with `0xAA, 0x55, 0x80, 0x10` and manipulates WAITCNT at
`0x04000204`. That is a **Flash save-media command sequence**: each write is a
hardware command, and repeated stores to the same address are the protocol, not
redundancy. Removing `volatile` changes the emitted size 116 → 104 and the count
0 → 53, because plain C lets the compiler coalesce those stores away — which
would also break the hardware.

So `volatile` here is mandatory semantics, not a matching device, and the
prohibition in §9 does not reach it. Two supporting facts: **127 already-adopted
`src/*.c` and 17 `assets/code/*.c` use `volatile`**, and §4 already licenses it
"only where the reference genuinely repeats the load".

The usable rule, given both cases landed on the same day:
**delete the `volatile` and re-probe.** If nothing changes it was noise, so drop
it (`080060e8`). If size and count both worsen, ask whether the address is
memory-mapped and whether repetition is the protocol; if yes it is semantics and
admissible (`08006cdc`), if no it is a matching device and the draft is not.

It also needed the `old-agbcc` family at `-O1` rather than the routed gcc 2.96
(0 versus 56 at the routed default). Its neighbours `08006c24`, `08006c68` and
`08006dec` are already `AGBCC_SOURCES`, so the whole region family is
agbcc-compiled and this is corroboration, not a lucky flag. Adopted via
`AGBCC_SOURCES` plus `AGBCC_OPTIMIZE_O1_SOURCES`.

**Routing-set self-tests will fail you three times, by design.** Adding stems
tripped `grouped DMA source allowlist`, `old_agbcc source allowlist`, and
`old_agbcc flags` self-tests in turn, each asserting a literal expected list.
That is the §7 "routing sets are easy to edit into the wrong one" guard working as
intended: update the assertion beside the set, and run `bun run test` before
`bun run verify`, because the test chain is the cheaper failure.

## The composed pipeline over every remaining clean draft

`tools/finish_draft.sh` was run across all 296 remaining compiling clean drafts.
**241 improved**, 106 are now size-exact, and **1 reached exact** (`0807a550`,

## Combining modes: ranking the singles first is wrong

`finish_draft.sh` originally expanded only the best-scoring single settings into
pairs. That is measurably the wrong rule, for the reason §6 already gives about
the CSE pair: **the winning combination is often built from modes that are
individually neutral or worse.**

- `08091174` reaches 3 with `-mgrouped-dma-store,-fno-cse-pool-immediate`, and
  neither flag is in its top six alone.
- `0800300c` reaches 2 with
  `-fno-cse-pool-immediate,-fno-schedule-insns2,-fthumb-group-control-last`,
  the same way, from a 52-halfword start at the wrong size.

Both were found by hand by drafting lanes and missed by the ranked expansion.
The fix is to pair exhaustively over a curated pool of modes known to interact
(the two CSE modes, the schedulers, the descriptor groupers, the immediate and
regmove modes) plus descriptor-family triples: 137 settings, a few seconds. With
that in place the tool reproduces both results mechanically.

## Second and third drafting tranches, and a lost-file lesson

Tranches 2 and 3 (12 and 14 lanes) produced **11 byte-exact regions** between
them. Adopted: `08078588` 144, `0800eaf8` 168, `0808bd24` 188, `080be0b4` 214,
`080c1df4` 200, `0807a1f8` 194, `080b1470` 204. Rejected: `080b386c` 212, exact
but on `volatile u16 slots[15]`, an ordinary stack array, which is the prohibited
matching-device case. Still outstanding at the time of writing: `0802281c` and
`080fa798`, whose lanes reported exact but whose sources were lost.

**Lanes writing to a shared output directory will destroy each other's work.**
Every lane was told to write `work/reprobe-2026-07-30/fresh/<stem>.c`. Between
tranches that directory was emptied of the earlier tranche's files, and five
confirmed-exact drafts vanished with it. Four were recovered only because the
*verify* lanes had copied them into their own scratch directories; two were not
recoverable and their regions remain unconverted despite having been solved.

Give each lane its own output directory (`fresh/<stem>/<stem>.c`), and adopt
confirmed results promptly rather than batching them across tranches. Bytes that
were measured exact and then lost are the most expensive possible outcome.

## OPEN QUESTION for a human: `08006878` and volatile delay loops

`08006878` (150 bytes) compiles byte-exact with `--family old-agbcc --flags -O1`
and is otherwise a clean, faithful implementation of a Flash chip-ID read: unlock
sequence to `0x0e005555`/`0x0e002aaa`, read the two ID bytes, reset sequence, with
a 20,000-iteration delay loop before and after. **It is not adopted, pending a
policy call.**

The `volatile` on the Flash writes is uncontroversial and matches `08006cdc`. The
problem is the delay loop, which uses `volatile u16 *w` pointing at an ordinary
**local**:

```c
w = &wait;
*w = 20000;
while (*w != 0) { s32 count = *w; count--; *w = count; }
```

Measured: exact with it, 59 halfwords and 14 bytes short with the local's
`volatile` removed (MMIO writes still volatile), 66 with all `volatile` removed.
So it is load-bearing.

Which way it goes depends on a distinction the rule in HANDOVER §9 does not yet
cover:

- **Argument to accept**: a busy-wait has no effect in C other than elapsed time,
  and `volatile` is the standard and essentially only portable way to stop the
  compiler deleting it. The repository's own retention taxonomy already contains
  `deliberate_busy_wait` (`080a8904` is one), so such loops are genuinely present
  in this ROM. Under that reading the `volatile` is semantics, exactly as it is
  for the Flash writes beside it.
- **Argument to reject**: it is `volatile` on an ordinary local, which is the same
  surface shape as `080b386c`'s `volatile u16 slots[15]` — rejected the same day.

The honest difference is that `080b386c` was preserving stores to a data buffer,
